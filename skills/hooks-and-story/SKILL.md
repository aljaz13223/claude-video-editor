# hooks-and-story

## The hook (first 1–3 s)
- Viewer must know instantly **what it is about (subject)** and **what they get (stakes)**.
- **4 S**: Subject clear · Stakes (why care) · Speed (fewest words — compress, don't talk faster) · Super clear (one reading, no wordplay).
- **Triple hook**, all saying the same thing: text on screen + visual (movement/pattern break) + spoken line.
- **Text hook** (most important): top third, never over eyes/mouth, max 2 lines, break on natural phrases, no punctuation
  (except quotes), no conjunctions, ≥ 3 s on screen, specific numbers beat general words.
- **Slippery lead-in** (ads especially): first 11–14 words jump into the action or the peak of the story, conversational,
  like a quote. Start at the climax, not at the beginning of the story.
- **Lock-in zone** (5–10 s after the hook): confirm the promise + trust (clean picture/sound, relatable, proof).

## Triple hook method (context → lean → snapback)
1. **Context** in the first line — said, shown and written. Even delaying it to the back half of the first sentence raises skips.
2. **Lean**: make them curious in one direction ("most people get step two wrong").
3. **Snapback**: a twist against the expectation the lean set up, then deliver.

## Instagram/TikTok safe zones
Keep captions and key text out of the right 15 % (like/comment/share column) and the bottom 20 % (caption + username),
and out of the top 10 %. Text may go *behind* the speaker's head (see motion-inserts `text_behind.py`) but never over the mouth.

## Choosing a clip from a long recording
One idea, 20–70 s, stands alone, first sentence is already a hook; prefer one continuous answer over stitched fragments;
personal stories and general ideas over accusations against people or groups. Speech starts within 0.4 s, ends on the last word.

## Story rhythm
- **Announce → face, describe → show**: when the speaker introduces a point, stay on the face; when they explain it, show it
  (insert, b-roll, card). This alternation is what keeps attention.
- One visual idea per sentence; full screen only for the biggest moments; a repeating element for chapters (#1/#2/#3).
- Lists: show the list early (blurred/partial = open loop), then each item gets its own beat; resolve with a callback to the hook.
- End: CTA only if the speaker says it; otherwise end on the strongest line + brand card.

## Carousel (reels = discovery, carousels = trust)
5 beats: hook → promise → pull → payoff → ask. Cover decides everything. 3 colours with fixed roles, 2 fonts, fixed layout.

## Value test (platforms demonetise templated content)
Every piece must have its own idea, its own hook and its own visuals — never the same template with new words.
If two clips would get the same title, keep the better one.
